Scope

What is included

Monitoring and alerting

Health, capacity and availability monitoring on the agreed scope, with alerts routed to people who are actually on duty. We tune the thresholds so alerts mean something — an alarm nobody trusts is worse than no alarm.

Patching and maintenance

Operating system and agreed platform-level patching on a defined cycle, applied to a staging environment first where one exists.

Backup and restore verification

We confirm backups are running, and we periodically restore from them. A backup that has never been restored is an assumption, not a backup.

Incident response

A defined escalation path and a named responder for incidents inside the agreed scope, with a written post-incident summary for anything significant.

Change management

Changes go through a documented request, review and rollback process. Small enough to not slow you down; formal enough that you know who changed what and when.

Access and identity hygiene

Periodic review of accounts, roles and keys — including the dormant admin account belonging to someone who left.

Monthly reporting

A monthly report covering availability, incidents, changes, patch status and spend, plus a quarterly review of what should change.

The commitment

What actually means

We commit to availability on environments under an agreed CloudOps scope. Because that number is worth very little without its definition, here is what sits behind it.

What is measured

The availability of the infrastructure and platform components inside the agreed scope, not your application code.

What it is not

A measured historical figure, or a commitment about a cloud partner's own platform availability. Partner platforms carry their own service commitments, which we will identify for your configuration.

The measurement window, the full exclusion list and the remedy are set out in the service agreement, and we will walk you through all three before you sign. An uptime figure without those three things attached is a slogan, not a commitment.

Boundaries

What is not included

Being clear about this up front is cheaper for both of us than discovering it during an incident.

  • Application code. We operate the platform your application runs on. We do not debug your application logic, though we will help you find out which side a problem is on.
  • Anything outside the agreed scope. The scope is written down and reviewed quarterly. Adding an environment is a conversation, not an assumption.
  • A security programme. Operational hygiene is included; a formal security programme, penetration testing and certification audit work are separate.
  • Unmanaged emergency work on environments we have not assessed. We cannot responsibly take on-call responsibility for an environment we have never seen.
