Fit

This is for you if

  • You are running production workloads on physical servers, a colocation rack, or a legacy hosting provider, and support for it is getting thinner every year.
  • You are on a cloud you chose years ago, and your users have since moved to markets it serves poorly.
  • You have been quoted a migration by a provider who also happens to sell only one cloud.
  • You need a presence in a specific market — Indonesia, Vietnam, mainland China — and your current platform cannot give you one.
  • Your last migration attempt stalled, and nobody wants to be the person who proposes trying again.

Scope

What is included

Discovery and dependency mapping

We inventory what you actually run — not what the documentation says you run. Servers, databases, scheduled jobs, integrations, certificates, DNS, licences, and the undocumented script on someone's laptop that turns out to be load-bearing.

Target platform selection

A written recommendation with the reasoning shown: where your users are, what latency they experience today, what your compliance and data-residency requirements are, what you are paying now, and what each candidate platform would cost. Because we are certified across six platforms, this recommendation is not predetermined.

Migration design

Wave planning, cut-over sequencing, data-synchronisation approach, DNS and traffic-shifting strategy, and a documented rollback procedure for each wave.

Execution

The migration itself, run in waves, starting with a non-critical workload wherever one exists. Cut-overs are scheduled in your maintenance windows, not ours.

Validation and handover

Post-migration verification against agreed criteria, updated architecture documentation, runbooks, and a handover session with your team. If you continue with CloudOps, the runbooks carry straight over.

Mainland China

Deploying inside mainland China

Serving users inside mainland China from a Singapore or US region usually produces latency your customers will notice. Deploying inside mainland China removes that problem, but the entry requirements are different from anywhere else in Asia-Pacific — and this is one of the areas where being certified on Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud and Huawei Cloud matters in practice, not just on a logo wall.

We will tell you early if your situation does not support a compliant China deployment. That is a cheaper conversation than finding out halfway through.

What this typically involves

  • A China-region deployment on a cloud that operates there under local licence.
  • ICP filing. A publicly accessible website or app served from within mainland China generally requires an ICP filing, which in turn generally requires a qualifying local entity.
  • Cross-border connectivity between your China-region workloads and everything you run elsewhere in Asia-Pacific.
  • A data-handling review covering what data crosses the border and what must stay put.
